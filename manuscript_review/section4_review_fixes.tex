\textcolor{blue}{
    The multi-scenario instance combines three market scenarios with three load and renewable-generation scenarios over five representative three-year blocks. The planning search again returned the plan without shared storage, $\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{0}$. As reported in Table~\ref{tab:res_multiscenario}, the reference unit provides an estimated discounted operational value of 236.7~k\euro{} against an investment cost of 318.0~k\euro{}. The resulting shortfall of 81.3~k\euro{} is 4.52 times the reported resolution of 18.0~k\euro{}, supporting the same economic conclusion as the single-scenario instance. These evaluations were stopped by the production exit of the coordination procedure (ten consecutive cycles passing the residual test with every local solve successful; Section~\ref{sec:case_settings}), rather than by the certification rule of Subsection~\ref{subsubsec:certification}.
}

\textcolor{blue}{
    To assess the sensitivity of the storage-value comparison to this stopping criterion, the evaluation of the plan without storage was continued beyond its stopping point. A subsequent fit to the objective trajectory estimated a further cost decrease of 6.2~k\euro{}. Accounting for this decrease changes the ratio of the reference unit's multi-scenario value to its settled single-scenario value from 0.934 to 0.909. The resulting range therefore reflects the estimated remaining movement of the no-storage evaluation; it is not a certification interval based on $\tau$. The ratio of 0.9331 estimated before the run from the mean-profile price spread lies within this range. Overall, the reference unit's estimated value is approximately 7--9\,\% lower in the multi-scenario instance, while its investment margin remains negative.
    This difference characterises the two evaluated instances and should not be attributed solely to scenario uncertainty, since their temporal discretisation and commitment-cost treatment also differ.
}

\textcolor{blue}{
    Deferring the reference unit does not make it pay (Table~\ref{tab:res_year_ladder}). Against the plan without storage, the unit installed in 2030 increases the total cost by 68.5~k\euro{} and the unit installed in 2035 by 111.0~k\euro{}. A later installation shortens the part of the horizon over which the unit operates, while the terminal salvage credit returns its remaining calendar life: 11.8~k\euro{} for the 2030 unit and 56.5~k\euro{} for the 2035 unit. The gross difference between the two deferral years, 42.5~k\euro{}, is determinate (3.46 times its threshold of 12.3~k\euro{}); net of salvage it is $-2.3$~k\euro{}, within resolution (0.19 times). In this instance, the choice between deferral years is therefore decided by the salvage convention rather than by operation; net of salvage, the unit costs 54.5--64.4~k\euro{} more than the plan without storage in every investment year.
}

\textcolor{blue}{
    The conclusion is also robust to the discount rate (Table~\ref{tab:res_discount}). The investment is paid in 2025 and the operational savings accrue over the horizon, so a higher rate lowers the value of the unit, from 277.1~k\euro{} undiscounted to 203.4~k\euro{} at 8\,\%. The unit does not pay at any rate between 0 and 8\,\%: its shortfall grows from 40.8~k\euro{} (2.57 times its threshold) to 114.6~k\euro{} (8.78 times), and every difference is determinate. Even without discounting, its operational savings over the horizon fall short of its investment cost.
}
